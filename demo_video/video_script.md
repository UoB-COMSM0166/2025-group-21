### Challenges

[Team / Tom voice over]
We faced a number of challenges throughout our development process:

1. Procedurally generated infinite map
- The first ‘must-have’ feature we decided on was an infinitely generated map. With the style of our game we needed the player to be able to go as far as they can, so we wanted the terrain to be randomly generated for as long as they can stay alive. But we also wanted the terrain to have variation, unpredictability and the ability to easily scale in difficulty. So, after lots of trial and error, we ended up pulling out some old trigonometry sine curves to get our desired result.

2. Physics and Motion
- We decided that for a game with lots of player and terrain interaction, we really needed it to feel responsive, fun and predictable. So, we spent a lot of time perfecting the way the penguin interacts with its environment. We started by coding in real life physics equations to dictate the penguin’s movement, depending on things like the gradient of the slope, the penguins current velocity, acceleration, the gravity. Then we tweaked these parameters, to make it feel as fun as possible.

3. Upgradability
- Since our game isn’t level based, we got a lot of feedback about having clear progression and a reason to keep playing. We decided that allowing upgrading and customising of the player was a great way to address this. We built an in game currency, which is earned by playing. And, can be spent in the shop, on upgradable items. This encourages and rewards players for playing more and getting better.

### Process

Throughout the development of our game we tried to incorporate a range of software engineering practices.

CI/CD
- Our group utilised a Continuous Integration / Continuous Deployment approach to keep our production game on the repo as up-to-date as possible.

Kanban
- We managed and tracked tasks on a Kanban board.

Sprint
- We ran two stages of sprints after assigning new tasks amongst the group, followed up with a sprint review, which helped us share how we were progressing.

Meetings
- On top of this, we had weekly meetings in-person and online where we shared ideas and perspectives on the game
